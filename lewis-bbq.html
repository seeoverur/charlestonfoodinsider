A Texas barbecue destination in Charleston’s upper peninsula

Lewis Barbecue serves Central Texas-style smoked meats at 464 North Nassau Street in Charleston. Pitmaster John Lewis brought his custom smokers to the Holy City in 2015 and opened the restaurant the following year. Today, guests order at the counter while brisket, pork ribs, turkey and sausage are sliced in front of them. The large dining room, full bar and backyard patio turn a tray of barbecue into a relaxed Charleston gathering that works for locals, visitors, families and groups.

Restaurant Spotlight

Lewis Barbecue Brings Central Texas Brisket to Charleston

A Different Barbecue Tradition

South Carolina has one of the country’s oldest barbecue traditions, and pork served with regional sauces remains an important part of the state’s food identity. Lewis Barbecue adds a different perspective to Charleston by concentrating on the meat and fire traditions of Central Texas. Beef brisket receives top billing, and the restaurant approaches it with the patience required to develop a dark bark, rendered fat and a tender interior.

The result is a barbecue restaurant that feels connected to Charleston’s casual dining culture while remaining unmistakably Texan. Trays are lined with butcher paper, meats are sold by weight, white bread and pickled vegetables accompany the order, and diners can decide whether they prefer lean slices, fatty slices or a mixture of both.

The Story Behind the Smoke

John Lewis grew up in El Paso and moved to Austin as a young adult. A birthday smoker helped begin his interest in backyard barbecue, and he later developed his skills through competition cooking and professional restaurant work. He became closely involved with some of the most influential names in Austin barbecue before bringing his smokers to Charleston.

Lewis moved to the Holy City in 2015 and opened the Charleston restaurant in June 2016. His hands-on approach extends beyond seasoning and cooking. The large offset smokers associated with the restaurant were designed and welded by the pitmaster, reflecting the idea that airflow, fire management and equipment construction all affect the final slice of brisket.

Brisket Is the Main Event

Sliced USDA Prime beef brisket is the restaurant’s signature item. Guests order as much or as little as they need, and the official menu recommends roughly one-half pound of meat per person as a general planning point. A first-time diner may want a combination of lean and fatty brisket to compare the firmer flat with the richer point.

Chopped brisket offers a different texture and is lightly mixed with the restaurant’s sauce. It can be ordered by weight, piled onto a sandwich or combined with sausage and pulled pork in the El Sancho. The sandwich format is convenient for lunch, but ordering meat by the pound gives a table more freedom to taste several selections.

Ribs, Sausage, Turkey and Pulled Pork

Brisket may lead the menu, but it should not automatically become the entire order. Pork spare ribs arrive lightly glazed and meaty, while pulled pork gives guests a familiar Southern barbecue option with a restrained amount of tangy sauce. The smoked turkey breast is another useful choice for diners who want something leaner without giving up the flavor of the pit.

House-made sausage adds some of the clearest Texas character. Texas Hot Guts combines mostly beef with some pork and a noticeable kick. Green chile cheddar sausage folds peppers and melted cheese into a pork link. Ordering one link for the table is an easy way to add another texture and flavor without committing to a large serving.

Sides That Complete the Tray

The sides at Lewis are designed to support the smoked meats rather than disappear beside them. Creamy potato salad provides a cool, familiar contrast. Tangy coleslaw uses an oil-and-vinegar dressing, and cowboy pinto beans bring Tex-Mex seasoning to the tray. Collard greens and mac and cheese connect the menu more directly to Southern comfort food.

Green chile corn pudding is one of the most recognizable side dishes. It resembles a soft, cheesy cornbread casserole with diced Hatch chiles that contribute flavor rather than aggressive heat. Beef-tallow-fried sidewinder fries, pickled onions, jalapeños and a hunk of cheddar provide additional ways to build the meal.

Sandwiches, Specials and Dessert

Diners who prefer a more compact order can choose sliced brisket, chopped brisket, pulled pork or turkey on a bun. The El Sancho combines chopped brisket or pulled pork with sausage, while the Loco version brings all three meats together. Nachos and chips with green chile queso turn smoked meat into a shareable starter or bar snack.

Lewis also promotes rotating daily specials. These may include beef back ribs, smoked prime rib, a barbecue Reuben, a beef-and-cheddar sandwich, giant beef short ribs or chicken-fried steak. Specials are often available only on a particular day and may sell out, so visitors should review the current official menu rather than assuming an item will be available.

Banana pudding and chocolate dirt pie offer a cool, sweet finish after a rich barbecue tray. Sharing dessert is often enough after brisket, sausage, sides and white bread, but both choices are built around familiar flavors that fit the casual setting.

A Relaxed Charleston Experience

Lewis Barbecue does not accept reservations. Guests join the counter line, select their meats and carry the finished tray to the dining area. The system allows diners to see the food being sliced and ask for the amount or brisket style they prefer. Lines can develop during popular meal periods, especially on weekends and when a limited special is offered.

The restaurant’s spacious layout makes the wait feel different from a small dining room. A large bar and backyard patio provide room for cold drinks, groups and an unhurried meal. Happy hour is currently posted for weekday afternoons, giving visitors another way to experience the bar and smaller food selections.

Worth the Trip for Barbecue Fans

Lewis Barbecue gives Charleston diners a focused introduction to Central Texas barbecue. The experience is built around fire management, custom smokers, cut-to-order meats and the confidence to let brisket remain the center of the tray. Order more than one meat, add green chile corn pudding and another contrasting side, then leave room for the patio and a cold drink. For barbecue fans visiting Charleston, Lewis is more than a quick lunch stop—it is a destination shaped by smoke, time and craft.

Menu Highlights

Build a balanced Lewis Barbecue tray

A strong first order combines at least two meats with one rich side, one brighter side and the traditional pickles, onions and bread.

Start with Brisket

Request a mixture of lean and fatty sliced brisket to experience the meat’s different textures and levels of richness.

Add a Second Meat

Choose pork spare ribs, smoked turkey, pulled pork or one of the house-made sausage links to create contrast.

Choose the Corn Pudding

Green chile corn pudding is a rich, cheesy signature side. Pair it with tangy slaw or pickled onions for balance.

Finish with a Cold Drink

The full bar, soft drinks and patio atmosphere make it easy to slow down after moving through the counter line.

Catering and Events

Bring Lewis Barbecue to a Charleston gathering

Lewis offers pickup and delivery for large orders, onsite gatherings at the restaurant and full-service offsite events. Family-style catering packages can include brisket, ribs, sausage, pulled pork, turkey, sides, bread, pickles, onions and barbecue sauces.

The catering team can also discuss different budgets, dietary restrictions and group sizes. Large pickup and delivery orders should be planned in advance rather than treated as a normal same-day takeout order.
