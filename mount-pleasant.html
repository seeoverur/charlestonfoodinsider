Mount Pleasant Dining Guide

A Charleston-area food destination with several distinct personalities

Mount Pleasant stretches from the Ravenel Bridge toward the beaches, creating a restaurant scene that is larger and more varied than many first-time visitors expect. Shem Creek supplies the classic waterfront experience, where fishing boats, boardwalks and marsh sunsets surround seafood restaurants and energetic outdoor bars. The Old Village offers a quieter atmosphere shaped by historic streets and neighborhood hospitality. Coleman Boulevard includes longtime Southern favorites, oyster bars, gastropubs and locally owned dining rooms, while newer residential areas support polished neighborhood restaurants away from the main tourist route. Charleston Food Insider created this guide as a curated starting point rather than a numerical ranking. The right restaurant depends on whether the occasion calls for brunch, a sunset drink, an anniversary dinner, a family meal or an easy night near the water.

The Local List

Ten Mount Pleasant restaurants worth knowing

These selections include waterfront landmarks, locally loved brunch spots and reservation-worthy dining rooms. Menus, opening hours and reservation policies can change, so confirm the latest details through each restaurant’s official website.

French Seafood Oyster Bar

NICO Oysters + Seafood

NICO brings French technique and Lowcountry ingredients together near Shem Creek. The lively restaurant is built around oysters, wood-fired seafood and a raw bar where diners can watch part of the preparation. It is a useful choice for visitors who want coastal food in a polished setting without the formality of a traditional fine-dining room. Order several oyster varieties, add a warm seafood preparation and allow the bar team to recommend a cocktail or wine pairing. The inside-outside layout helps NICO feel connected to the creek while remaining comfortable in changing weather. Reservations are recommended for dinner, especially on weekends and during popular Charleston travel seasons.

Seasonal American Creekside Dining

Tavern & Table

Tavern & Table pairs one of Mount Pleasant’s best-known waterfront settings with a chef-driven menu and a strong beverage program. The restaurant works equally well for a leisurely lunch, sunset drinks or a full dinner with friends. Seasonal ingredients and straightforward preparations keep the food approachable, while the indoor and outdoor bars give the property an energetic Shem Creek atmosphere. Groups can share starters before moving into entrées, and the cocktail list adds another reason to stay through sunset. Because waterfront tables are naturally in high demand, guests should reserve when possible and understand that a specific outdoor seat may depend on weather and availability.

Seafood & Barbecue Sunset Hangout

Saltwater Cowboys

Saltwater Cowboys combines two crowd-friendly styles—Lowcountry seafood and slow-smoked barbecue—on a large deck overlooking Shem Creek. This variety makes the restaurant practical for groups in which some guests want shrimp or oysters while others prefer brisket, pork or casual bar food. The setting is relaxed, social and often lively, with cocktails, sunset views and scheduled music adding to the experience. Families and dog owners may appreciate the outdoor space, although current policies should always be confirmed. Saltwater Cowboys becomes especially busy during warm weekends, so arrive earlier than the ideal sunset time and allow room in the schedule for parking and a possible wait.

Southern & Lowcountry Breakfast Favorite

Page’s Okra Grill

Page’s Okra Grill is one of Mount Pleasant’s best-known destinations for generous Southern cooking. The family-owned restaurant serves breakfast, brunch, lunch and dinner, with made-from-scratch biscuits, fried chicken, shrimp and grits, vegetables and creative comfort-food combinations shaping its identity. It is an excellent introduction for visitors who want familiar Lowcountry flavors in a casual setting. Weekend brunch can create a crowd, so an early arrival is often more comfortable than appearing at the busiest late-morning hour. Portions are substantial, making sharing useful for groups that want to sample several dishes. The covered outdoor area adds space and keeps the restaurant feeling lively.

Creative Southern Grits & Bourbon

Grace & Grit

Grace & Grit gives Southern food a creative, chef-led presentation while maintaining the generous hospitality expected from a neighborhood restaurant. The menu places fresh seafood, local produce and flavored grits at the center of the experience. Its well-known grit flight allows newcomers to compare multiple preparations rather than selecting only one. A carefully developed bourbon program makes the restaurant equally appealing for drinks and dinner. Grace & Grit is suitable for brunch, celebrations and visitors who want a modern Lowcountry meal away from the busiest creekside decks. Review the weekly schedule because brunch and dinner service vary by day, and reserve ahead when visiting with a group.

Coastal Tavern Historic Old Village

Post House

Post House is a coastal tavern and boutique inn in Mount Pleasant’s historic Old Village. Its location, seasonal menu and classic cocktails create an experience that feels connected to neighborhood life rather than a conventional commercial district. The dining room works for lunch, brunch, dinner, date nights and special occasions, while the surrounding streets encourage a walk before the reservation. Post House is particularly appealing to guests who appreciate intimate design, thoughtful seafood and a slower style of hospitality. Demand can be strong because both residents and Charleston visitors seek out the restaurant, so reservations are the safest choice. Leave time to enjoy the Old Village instead of treating the meal as a quick stop.

Lowcountry Fine Dining Anniversary Dinner

Langdon’s Restaurant & Wine Bar

Langdon’s is one of Mount Pleasant’s established fine-dining restaurants, combining Lowcountry ingredients with broader global influences. The refined room, extensive wine options and attentive service make it appropriate for anniversaries, milestone birthdays and business dinners. This is a restaurant for diners who want to spend time over the meal rather than rush toward another activity. Seafood and meat preparations receive equal attention, which helps mixed groups find suitable choices. Ask the service team for guidance when pairing wine or planning multiple courses. Langdon’s is located away from the main Shem Creek crowds, offering a quieter alternative when the occasion matters more than a waterfront view.

American & Seafood Casual Waterfront

Vickery’s Bar & Grill

Vickery’s is a longtime Shem Creek restaurant known for broad water views and a comfortable, unpretentious atmosphere. Its menu mixes seafood, sandwiches, salads and familiar American dishes, making the restaurant useful for families and groups that do not want a narrow concept. The large space can accommodate more guests than some smaller creekside restaurants, but popular times still create waits. Vickery’s is a good choice for lunch after walking the boardwalk or for an early dinner before Shem Creek nightlife becomes busier. Choose it when the goal is a recognizable waterfront experience with enough menu variety to satisfy adults and children.

Gastropub Craft Beer

Coleman Public House

Coleman Public House is a neighborhood gastropub where craft beer, burgers, sandwiches and elevated comfort food share the menu. It provides an easy alternative to seafood-heavy dining and works well for lunch, casual dinner or drinks with friends. The setting feels local and relaxed, making it useful for visitors who want to step away from the tourist-focused waterfront for one meal. Beer drinkers can explore a rotating selection while the kitchen offers enough variety for guests who prefer wine, cocktails or nonalcoholic options. Coleman Public House is especially convenient when exploring the businesses along Coleman Boulevard or planning an informal gathering without a lengthy fine-dining service.

Know Mount Pleasant

Choose a restaurant by dining area

Mount Pleasant covers a large area, and traffic near the Ravenel Bridge, beaches and major shopping corridors can affect travel time. Grouping dinner with the neighborhood you already plan to explore keeps the day relaxed.

Shem Creek

NICO, Tavern & Table, Saltwater Cowboys and Vickery’s place diners near the creek, shrimp boats and boardwalk. Visit for waterfront atmosphere, but prepare for parking pressure and heavy weekend crowds.

Old Village

Post House anchors the historic village dining experience. Combine a reservation with a walk along Pitt Street, quiet residential blocks and waterfront views near the Pitt Street Bridge area.

Coleman Boulevard

Page’s Okra Grill and Coleman Public House provide contrasting Southern and gastropub experiences. This corridor is convenient for visitors crossing from downtown Charleston or traveling toward Sullivan’s Island.

I’On & Central Mount Pleasant

Community Table and nearby neighborhood restaurants offer a quieter experience, while Grace & Grit and Langdon’s provide reservation-worthy meals away from the busiest tourist zones.

Before you dine

Practical Mount Pleasant restaurant tips

Shem Creek is most crowded near sunset, particularly from Thursday through Sunday. Arrive well before the exact sunset time if outdoor seating matters. Parking areas can fill, and moving between several waterfront lots may take longer than expected. A rideshare can reduce parking stress, but pickup times may increase at the end of the evening.

Weekend brunch is another popular period. Page’s Okra Grill, Grace & Grit and other daytime restaurants may develop waits after the early morning window. Groups should arrive together and review each restaurant’s seating policy in advance.

Mount Pleasant is also larger than it appears on a Charleston-area map. A restaurant near northern Highway 17 may be a meaningful drive from the Old Village or Ravenel Bridge during rush hour. Check the route before booking, allow time for bridge traffic and avoid scheduling dinner immediately after an activity on the opposite side of town.
